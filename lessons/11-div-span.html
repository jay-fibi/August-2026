div vs span

Two generic containers: div is a block-level box that stacks vertically; span is inline and sits within a line of text.

Lesson 11 Beginner

💡 The Concept

  • Block level (div) — takes the full width available and starts on a new line. Perfect for grouping sections, cards and columns.
  • Inline (span) — only as wide as its content and flows with surrounding text. Perfect for highlighting a word or styling a fragment.

Both are "meaningless" containers — they add no semantics, which is why you should prefer semantic elements (lesson 10) when a real meaning exists, and use div/span only as a fallback for grouping and styling.

💡 Tip: Don't nest divs forever. When you find a div holding a heading and a paragraph, consider whether article or section says it better.

Resize the window and you'll see each div always spans the full width, while the span stays glued to its sentence.

✅ Key Takeaways

Remember

  • div is a block container — full width, stacks vertically.
  • span is inline — flows within a line of text.
  • Both add no meaning; prefer semantic elements when they fit.
  • Use them for grouping, styling hooks and layout wrappers.
